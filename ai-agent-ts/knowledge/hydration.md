# **Key facts**

- Water is essential for nearly every bodily function, and getting enough fluid throughout the day supports normal physical and cognitive function.
- There is no single daily water-intake figure that applies to everyone – needs vary by age, sex, pregnancy/breastfeeding status, activity level, and climate.
- Water has no calories, so replacing sugary drinks with water is one of the simplest ways to reduce excess caloric intake.
- Foods with high water content, such as many fruits and vegetables, also contribute meaningfully to daily fluid intake – water doesn't have to come from drinks alone.
- Mild dehydration can affect mood, concentration and physical performance well before someone feels seriously thirsty.

---

# **Overview**

Staying adequately hydrated supports circulation, temperature regulation, digestion and kidney function, among other roles. Because individual fluid needs vary so widely, public health guidance generally avoids prescribing one fixed daily target and instead focuses on practical habits: drinking water regularly through the day, choosing water over sugar-sweetened beverages by default, and paying attention to fluid needs during hot weather, illness or increased physical activity.

# **CDC guidance on water and healthier drinks**

### **Daily fluid needs**

Recommended fluid intake is not a single universal number. It depends on factors including age, sex, pregnancy or breastfeeding status, activity level and climate. Rather than targeting a rigid figure, a reasonable approach is to drink water consistently throughout the day and increase intake during exercise, heat, or illness.

### **Signs of dehydration**

Not getting enough fluid can cause unclear thinking, mood changes, overheating, constipation and, in more serious cases, kidney stones. These effects can begin before a person feels strongly thirsty, which is why regular fluid intake throughout the day is preferable to drinking only when thirst is noticeable.

### **Healthier drink choices**

- Plain water, sparkling water and seltzers are calorie-free choices.
- Plain coffee or tea (without significant added sugar) can also contribute to daily fluid intake.
- Milk, and unsweetened fortified milk alternatives, contribute fluid along with other nutrients.
- Limited amounts of 100% fruit juice or low-sodium vegetable juice can be included, but whole fruits and vegetables are generally preferable to juice.
- Sugar-sweetened beverages (soda, sweetened coffee/tea drinks, sports drinks used outside of prolonged intense exercise) add calories with limited nutritional value and are best limited.

### **Practical tips**

Carrying a reusable water bottle, choosing water as the default beverage, and serving water alongside meals are simple ways to support consistent hydration without needing to track an exact daily volume.
